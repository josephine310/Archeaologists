
## Research Agenda

We are taking an exploratory approach. Our aim is to provide a clear picture of how the field of archaeology is structured and how it changes over time. In doing so, we refer to Pierre Bourdieu’s concepts of “field” and “habitus”
We look at who the people in the field,  their social background, their country of origin, their gender,the generation in which they were born into and what they think and argue. We also examine which organisations they belong to and how people and organisations are interlinked with each other. We aim to show who does what and where, and to what extent this varies across countries, groups and genders. We will then attempt to develop a model of how this structure changes over time.
We are using a prosopographic method, we collect the same types of data on many individuals in order to identify common patterns in their lives and professional careers.


## Research questions

* Do career paths differ depending on social background, country of origin, gender or generation?
* Who works where? Which countries do archaeologists come from, and in which countries do they carry out excavations or work? Are there differences between local and foreign researchers?
* How does gender affect careers and success (education, profession, fieldwork, funding, leadership positions, recognition)? To what extent does it overlap or interact with social class and country of origin?
* Which professions and roles (professors, museum staff, excavation directors, field researchers, illustrators, collectors, amateurs) are associated with particular backgrounds, countries or genders? Which roles are held in higher regard?
* Whose work remains invisible? Are certain people (for example, women, local workers or assistants) excluded from publications and the attribution of credit?
* How have memberships of organisations (academies, societies, museums, institutes, excavation projects) and informal groups changed over time? Who was allowed to join, and who was not?
* How are organisations connected, and how have these connections changed? Are there links between countries, such as colonial or international relations?
* How do background, country of origin and gender influence what is published (type of work, topics, publishers, order of authors), and how are they shaped by these factors? Who publishes what, in which language and with what level of visibility?



## Aspects of the information to be collected
* Gender, family situation, and country and place of origin
* Parents’ jobs and social background
* Education (schools, degrees, teachers)
* Teaching and archaeological work (excavations, museum work, lectures)
* Fieldwork: where, with whom, and in what role (director, participant, documenter)
* Other jobs during their life
* Memberships in institutions and the positions held there
* Prizes, funding, and other forms of recognition
* Activities outside of work
* Publications: topics, language, and authorship
